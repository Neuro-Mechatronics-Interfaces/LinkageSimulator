\section{Scope and conventions}

The application is a deterministic planar kinematics simulator with a bounded
quasi-static gravity estimate, not a dynamic, clinical, or musculoskeletal
model. The serial finger abstraction follows established hand kinematic
modelling practice \cite{an1979normative,buchholz1992anthropometric}. World
coordinates use millimetre-like units, angles use radians internally, and
positive angles are counter-clockwise. A rigid link has pose
$(\mathbf{p},\theta)$, and an arbitrary local point $\mathbf{q}$ maps to world
space as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p},\theta,\mathbf{q})
  =\mathbf{p}+R(\theta)\mathbf{q}, \qquad
  R(\theta)=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
    \sin\theta& \cos\theta
  \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The rectangular length and width are rendering and editing dimensions; joint
attachments are not required to lie at $\pm L/2$. Geometry and mechanism
solving have no DOM or Canvas dependency, and rendering does not mutate state.

\section{Independent digit workspaces}

The application instantiates independent simulation states for D2 through D5
and renders only the active tab. Servo angle, animation direction, linkage
edits, contactors, joint-limit status, and static-moment results therefore
persist independently for each digit. A shared hand-size factor $h$ applies to
all four anatomical models. Neutral initialization uses rounded between-digit
scales
\begin{equation}
  (s_2,s_3,s_4,s_5)=(1.00,1.11,1.03,0.83),
\end{equation}
consistent with reported young-adult mean digit lengths of approximately
$63.9$, $70.7$, $65.5$, and $53.3\,\mathrm{mm}$, respectively
\cite{chatzioglou2024biometric}. For base D2 segment length $L_{i,2}$, digit
$d$ is initialized with $L_{i,d}=h s_d L_{i,2}$. The linkage template is scaled
by $s_d$ at creation; subsequent hardware edits remain local to one digit.

\section{Dorsal mounting and actuator convention}

Let $\mathbf{G}$ be a point on the dorsal ground plane, with tangent
$\mathbf{t}_g$ and dorsal normal $\mathbf{n}_g$. The editable servo--ground
offset $h_g$ places the servo axis at
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{A}=\mathbf{G}+h_g\mathbf{n}_g.
\end{equation}
The fixed dorsal rail has length $L_g$ and stored orientation $\alpha_g$, so its
second pivot is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{D}=\mathbf{A}+L_g(\cos\alpha_g,\sin\alpha_g).
\end{equation}
The rail is identified through the servo's referenced revolute joint rather
than a reserved link name.

The primary servo command $\phi$ preserves the original application semantics:
it is the absolute world orientation of the driven link, not an angle relative
to the rail. If $\mathbf{q}_s$ is the driven link's local point at the servo
revolute, the actuator seed is
\begin{equation}
  \theta_s=\phi, \qquad
  \mathbf{p}_s=\mathbf{A}-R(\phi)\mathbf{q}_s.
\end{equation}
The animation advances $\phi$ between its configured limits and reverses at
each bound. Actuator mobility analysis adds the equality
$\operatorname{wrap}(\theta_s-\phi)=0$; passive analysis omits it.

\section{Constraint graph and connected components}

Every rigid link is a graph body, together with a world body. A fixed link is
connected to world and contributes no configuration variables. Each revolute
joint is an edge between two link bodies or between a link and world. A
zero-range revolute adds a locked relative-angle edge, and the servo contributes
an actuator edge referencing its driven revolute joint. Component discovery is
based on adjacency and is independent of IDs and array insertion order.

For each connected component the analysis records its links, joints,
actuators, connection to fixed/world geometry, unresolved links, scalar
residual count, Jacobian ranks, and local mobilities. A component without a
world or fixed connection is reported as free-floating. Its three global rigid
motions are retained; it is never silently attached to world. Multiple
components are analyzed separately.

Figure~\ref{fig:solver-flow} shows the update path. Analytic propagation remains
the preferred solver. Numerical minimization is reached only for an unresolved
subsystem that is still constrained rather than genuinely free.

\section{Residual equations, rank, and local mobility}

Each nonfixed planar link contributes
$\mathbf{q}_i=(x_i,y_i,\theta_i)$ to the component configuration vector. For a
revolute joint $j$ between bodies $A$ and $B$, with local attachment points
$\mathbf{a}_j$ and $\mathbf{b}_j$, the two closure residuals are
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{r}_j(\mathbf{q})=
  \mathbf{p}_A+R(\theta_A)\mathbf{a}_j
  -\mathbf{p}_B-R(\theta_B)\mathbf{b}_j.
  \label{eq:revolute}
\end{equation}
For a world-side joint the corresponding ground point replaces the first body
term. A locked revolute adds the wrapped angular residual
\begin{equation}
  r^{\theta}_j=\ell_{\theta}
  \operatorname{wrap}(\theta_B-\theta_A-\bar\theta_j),
\end{equation}
where $\ell_{\theta}=50\,\mathrm{mm}$ converts radians to a length-like scale.
This characteristic length is comparable to the demonstrator links and avoids
combining angular and positional residuals without scaling. The prescribed
actuator residual uses the same scale.

For stacked residual vector $\mathbf{r}$, a central finite-difference Jacobian
approximates
\begin{equation}
  J(\mathbf{q})=\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\mathbf{q}}.
\end{equation}
Scaled elimination with named absolute and relative pivot tolerances estimates
the rank. The local mobility is
\begin{equation}
  M_{\mathrm{local}}=n_q-\operatorname{rank}J.
  \label{eq:mobility}
\end{equation}
The calculation is performed once without the actuator equality and once with
the current command prescribed. Rank, rather than raw equation count, is the
informative local diagnostic when constraints are dependent.

At a regular default pose there are six movable links and therefore
$n_q=18$. Seven revolutes contribute 14 positional residuals, and the locked
dorsal-anchor joint contributes one angular residual. The passive Jacobian has
rank 15, giving $M_{\mathrm{passive}}=3$. Adding the absolute servo-orientation
equality raises the rank to 16 and gives $M_{\mathrm{driven}}=2$. The classical
four-bar subgraph accounts for one passive coordinate and becomes zero-DOF
when driven. The two remaining coordinates belong to the middle and distal
driver joints and are resolved later by finger/contact constraints.

\section{Analytic propagation}

The solver first marks fixed links resolved and constructs the servo-driven
pose from its referenced joint. It then repeats bounded graph passes until no
operation makes progress.

\paragraph{Two-point rigid reconstruction.}
Suppose local points $\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2$ on one unresolved body have
known world positions $\mathbf{x}_1,\mathbf{x}_2$. After checking
$\|\mathbf{x}_2-\mathbf{x}_1\|\simeq
 \|\mathbf{q}_2-\mathbf{q}_1\|$, the pose is
\begin{align}
  \theta &={\rm atan2}(x_{2y}-x_{1y},x_{2x}-x_{1x})
           -{\rm atan2}(q_{2y}-q_{1y},q_{2x}-q_{1x}),\\
  \mathbf{p}&=\mathbf{x}_1-R(\theta)\mathbf{q}_1.
\end{align}
Coincident local points are degenerate, and incompatible local/world distances
are inconsistent. Link length is not changed by reconstruction.

\paragraph{Locked-angle propagation.}
If one body at a revolute is resolved and
$\theta_B-\theta_A=\bar\theta_j$ is locked, the other orientation follows
directly. Its translation then places its own local attachment at the known
world joint. This graph rule replaces the former special dorsal-anchor angle.

\paragraph{Topology-discovered dyads.}
Two unresolved bodies form an analytic dyad when they share a revolute joint
$X$ and each has one other revolute attached to resolved geometry. Let the
known outer joint positions be $\mathbf{c}_1,\mathbf{c}_2$. The circle radii
come from local attachment geometry, not rectangle length:
\begin{equation}
  \rho_1=\|\mathbf{q}_{X,1}-\mathbf{q}_{O,1}\|,
  \qquad
  \rho_2=\|\mathbf{q}_{X,2}-\mathbf{q}_{O,2}\|.
\end{equation}
The shared point satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \|\mathbf{x}_X-\mathbf{c}_1\|=\rho_1,
  \qquad
  \|\mathbf{x}_X-\mathbf{c}_2\|=\rho_2.
\end{equation}
The robust circle routine distinguishes two intersections, tangency, separation,
containment, coincidence, concentricity, and invalid radii. Each candidate is
reconstructed through the general two-point transform and checked against
ordinary joint ROM inequalities before acceptance.

When two candidates remain, the selected branch minimizes distance to the
preceding valid world position of joint $X$. This history is a transient map
keyed by arbitrary joint ID. If no valid snapshot exists, the current initial
joint position supplies the preference. Analytic steps retain their circle
centres, radii, candidates, and selected point for optional construction
rendering; rendering only reads this trace.

\section{Singularity and constraint diagnostics}

A tangent dyad is a branch-merging analytic singularity. Coincident circles are
degenerate and underdetermined rather than a unique closure. Independently, a
drop in the locally expected Jacobian rank marks a singular or near-singular
component. Zero mobility by itself is not singularity.

Three other conditions remain distinct. For $m$ scalar residuals and local rank
$r$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a structural overconstraint flag identifies a subsystem with more
    required scalar equalities than available variables;
  \item $m-r>0$ identifies dependent or redundant residual rows at the sampled
    pose, which need not invalidate a consistent mechanism; and
  \item geometric inconsistency means the closure norm cannot be reduced below
    tolerance.
\end{itemize}
Diagnostics report component membership, passive and driven mobility, rank,
unresolved links, residual norm, analytic solve count, numerical-fallback use,
and explanatory messages. Disconnected or intentionally free components may
therefore be valid while still carrying useful warnings.

\section{Bounded numerical fallback}

After analytic propagation reaches a fixed point, rank analysis decides whether
an unresolved subsystem is genuinely free or expected to close. Free
coordinates preserve their finite prior values and do not enter an optimizer.
For a constrained remainder, the initial condition combines the current partial
analytic result with the preceding valid coordinates. A damped
Gauss--Newton/Levenberg--Marquardt step solves
\begin{equation}
  \left(J^{\mathsf T}J+\lambda D\right)\Delta\mathbf{q}
  =-J^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{r},
  \label{eq:damped-step}
\end{equation}
where $D$ provides diagonal scale regularization. Central differences construct
$J$; translation and angular increments have separate caps. A bounded line
search accepts only finite, residual-decreasing candidates. Joint-ROM violation
residuals are inactive inside their intervals and active beyond a bound.

The iteration has a finite cap, exits early below closure tolerance, and reports
failure rather than committing a nonfinite or high-residual pose. Numerical
minimization is thus a local kinematic fallback, not dynamics, branch search, or
a device for hiding mobility.

\section{Simultaneous finger and contactor solve}

The finger remains a serial proximal--middle--distal chain downstream of the
general mechanism graph. DIP flexion is coupled to PIP flexion by
$\gamma=k\beta$, with $k=0.62$. For contactor $j$, let
$\mathbf{F}_j(\alpha,\beta)$ be its point on the finger centreline and
$\mathbf{n}_{d,j}$ the segment normal directed toward positive world $Y$. Let
$W_j$ be total internal ring width. Its admissible domain and per-side
affordance are
\begin{equation}
  W_j\geq w_{f,j},\qquad a_j=\frac{W_j-w_{f,j}}{2}\geq0.
\end{equation}
The linkage centreline target is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{T}_j=\mathbf{F}_j+
  \left(\frac{W_j}{2}+\frac{w_{l,j}}{2}\right)\mathbf{n}_{d,j}.
\end{equation}
The specialized default solve uses
$\mathbf{z}=(\alpha,\beta,\eta_m,\eta_d)$: MCP and PIP angles plus the two
passive driver angles. With linkage contact points $\mathbf{P}_m$ and
$\mathbf{P}_d$, it minimizes
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{r}_c(\mathbf{z})=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \mathbf{P}_m-\mathbf{T}_m\\
    \mathbf{P}_d-\mathbf{T}_d
  \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
A projected damped iteration enforces hand and driver limits. If one variable
reaches a bound, available variables continue solving. This stage consumes the
already-resolved upstream linkage and does not invoke a four-bar solver.

Every rectangular link is then tested against each phalanx capsule, except for
the link carrying the contactor assigned to that phalanx. For other pairs,
centreline segments $S_l,S_f$, widths $w_l,w_f$, and clearance $\epsilon$, the
pose requires
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{dist}(S_l,S_f)\geq\frac{w_l+w_f}{2}+\epsilon.
\end{equation}
Ground-plane and dorsal-rail crossings are also rejected. Failure restores the
last valid mechanism, hand, mount, contactor, and joint-limit state.

\section{Gravity-only static moments}

Each phalanx is approximated as a uniform cylinder, consistent with published
index-finger and hand parameterizations
\cite{qiu2014orthopaedic,tsakonas2026handmass}. For rendered length $L_i$,
diameter $d_i$, and tissue density $\rho=1160\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, mass is
\begin{equation}
  m_i=\rho\pi\left(\frac{d_i}{2}\right)^2L_i.
\end{equation}
With gravity $\mathbf{g}=(0,-g)$ and joint point $\mathbf{J}_k$, the world
gravity moment from distal segments is
\begin{equation}
  \tau^{g,z}_k=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}(k)}
  (\mathbf{r}_{i,k}\times m_i\mathbf{g})_z,
  \qquad \mathbf{r}_{i,k}=\mathbf{C}_i-\mathbf{J}_k.
\end{equation}
An optional torsional term has form
$\tau^s_k=-K_k(q_k-q_{0,k})$, with default $K_k=0$, and the displayed holding
moment is $\tau^{hold}_k=-(\tau^g_k+\tau^s_k)$. It excludes tendon forces,
contact forces, friction, and joint reactions \cite{tsakonas2024parameter}.

\section{Interaction, construction geometry, and export}

Dragging the driven-link handle converts the pointer direction into the
servo's absolute angle. Other actuator-connected handles sample the finite
primary-servo range and retain the valid pose with the nearest endpoint. A
genuinely free attached link rotates about its revolute point. This remains a
bounded one-coordinate inverse control rather than a general drag constraint.

The construction layer retains ordinary linkage cues and consumes the latest
analytic trace. For each discovered dyad it can render both circle loci and the
selected shared-joint intersection. These values originate in the simulation
stage; Canvas rendering remains read-only.

The JSON exporter serializes a schema name and version, timestamp, units,
shared hand scale, active tab, and detached D2--D5 persistent states. Solver
diagnostics, analytic trace, and prior-joint branch history are transient and
are omitted. Angles remain radians and lengths remain millimetres.

\section{Update ordering and invariants}

The frame update is ordered as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item advance or accept the absolute servo command;
  \item synchronize editable dorsal mounting geometry;
  \item build and analyze the constraint graph;
  \item run analytic propagation and, only when required, numerical fallback;
  \item solve the specialized finger/contact constraints;
  \item validate collisions and hard geometric exclusions;
  \item calculate finger statics;
  \item capture the valid finite snapshot and render it.
\end{enumerate}
Every committed revolute satisfies Equation~\eqref{eq:revolute} within named
closure tolerance. All link positions, angles, and residuals must be finite.
Analytic branches use only prior valid joint history, and a failed candidate
cannot replace that snapshot.

\section{Remaining mathematical scope}

The graph presently models revolute planar mechanisms with one prescribed
rotational servo. It has no prismatic joints, multiple independent actuator
commands, compliance, friction, rigid-body dynamics, or force-balanced contact.
The numerical fallback is local and does not guarantee a global solution or
deliberate assembly-branch transition. The two finger contactors remain a
specialized downstream solve rather than arbitrary graph constraints.

The next coherent milestone is to express arbitrary contactors and finger
coordinates in the same scaled residual formulation. That would expose their
contribution to rank and mobility directly and prepare the solver for generalized
direct manipulation and mechanism-dimension optimization.
